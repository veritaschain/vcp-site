Start with one workflow

For AI providers, engineering owners, security teams and governance teams. Choose one operation and one question: an agent updating a customer record, a business feature calling an external API, or an AI action requiring approval. These are examples for choosing a scope, not customer deployments.

Commercial reviews are provided through VeritasChain Co., Ltd. The public scope described by Tokachi Kamimura covers architecture and code review, agreed controlled testing, findings linked to evidence and explicit unresolved questions. Agree the materials and how to share them

A non-confidential summary is enough for the initial inquiry. Do not attach private keys, API tokens, customer information or raw conversation and execution logs. Agree the necessary materials, authorized readers, sharing channel and storage/deletion conditions with the review scope.

  • Target and owner: the operation, participating systems, environment owner and excluded systems.
  • Architecture and code: data and authority flows, source revision to examine, dependencies and relevant configuration descriptions.
  • Expected behavior: what should proceed or stop after approval, denial, recording failure or an unknown outcome.
  • Review material: sanitized records, synthetic inputs, error examples and the controlled environment available for testing.

If material is missing, start by documenting the gap and its effect on the questions that can be answered. Missing evidence does not establish the absence of a problem. For product-specific public information, see security and data handling.

Agree findings that can be traced to evidence

This is a candidate deliverable structure. Included items, level of detail, format and follow-up work are agreed for each engagement.

  • Scope record: reviewed revisions, environment, operations and period, with explicit exclusions.
  • Architecture and authority map: relationships between approval, tool execution and observation, and the boundaries examined.
  • Evidence-linked findings: observed facts, referenced code or records, affected conditions and unresolved points.
  • Agreed test record: inputs, steps, expectations, actual results and differences; distinguish tests that were not run.
  • Improvement candidates: proposed changes, reasons for priority and conditions to recheck. Implementation and operational changes require a separately agreed scope.

Illustrative finding, not a real assessment: “The mock update has a sending record, but no status-query record after the response was lost. The API outcome is unknown; completion and failure are both unestablished. Review the reconciliation and retry conditions.” This demonstrates a reporting format rather than a result for an actual system.

From inquiry to an agreed review

  1. Share an outline: the workflow, question, intended deliverables and preferred timeframe.
  2. Agree the engagement: feasibility, included and excluded scope, materials, deliverables, fees, schedule and handling conditions. An inquiry does not itself confirm a start date or delivery commitment.
  3. Review architecture and code: relate authority, execution and evidence using the agreed materials and revisions.
  4. Perform agreed testing when needed: confirm authorization for the controlled environment, targets, operations, executor and stop conditions. Unauthorized testing of third-party production services is outside this scope.
  5. Discuss findings and unknowns: separate observations from inferences and agree any further checks or retesting.

Even in a customer-managed environment, record who prepared, executed, changed settings or assisted. Execution or substantial assistance by VSO is not independent third-party reproduction.

Separate a technical review from research status

This page explains an inquiry process. It does not change normative requirements, existing implementations, signed data, fixed distributions, K3 or historical test records.

Experimental extensions and proposed repairs remain unadopted; independent third-party reproduction has not been established. Distinguish first-party CI, internal synthetic tests, saved-evidence rechecks and fresh execution in an independent environment. Conducting a commercial technical review does not itself establish independent VAP reproduction or formal adoption.

A technical review is not legal advice or conformity certification. It does not guarantee discovery of every vulnerability, production safety or capture of every action. A valid record signature alone does not establish the truth of its content or authorization for the recorded action.
