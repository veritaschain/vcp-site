For teams receiving photos and proofs

This guide helps insurance intake, field-record and technical support teams handle a photo and its VeraSnap proof consistently. Keep cryptographic findings separate from findings about an incident, damage or a payment decision. This is a recommended receiving workflow, not a new normative requirement or an insurance claims rule.

This page has no file-upload facility. Use your organization's approved intake and storage channels. Technical operators can follow the Verify SDK execution guide and choose a revision from the versions, distributions and known issues page.

The SDK is a first-party alpha implementation. Experimental extensions and proposed repairs remain unadopted; independent third-party reproduction has not been established.

1. Preserve the original and intake context

  1. Receive the photo, proof and accompanying explanation separately. Record the image file, corresponding shareable proof JSON or export ZIP, purpose and subject of submission. A photo alone is not receipt of its proof.
  2. Do not transform the original. Save receipt time with time zone, filename, byte count, SHA-256, receiving channel and handler; create a working copy. Compression, resaving and screenshots can change the hash.
  3. Separate statements from verified evidence. Do not relabel assertions about the subject, time, location or person as cryptographically established facts. Avoid duplicating unnecessary personal data in the working record.
  4. Record verification conditions. Keep the full SDK commit SHA, dependency record, key-registry identifier and applicable policy, trusted TSA roots and verification time. Do not attach private keys or credentials to the record.

A receipt-time SHA-256 helps identify the same bytes later. By itself, it does not establish the sender's identity or the history before receipt.

2. Supply the photo and read individual checks

  1. Keep the originals while a technical operator verifies working copies with a pinned SDK revision. If the task is to check a photo, supply the photo as well as its proof.
  2. Save the exit code, standard error and JSON result. Read media_verified and every check's id, status and code, alongside overall status and level.
  3. Check how the signing key was enrolled. known_unattested means manual registration. known identifies an active key treated as attested in the supplied registry; it does not mean that enrollment-time hardware evidence was reverified during this proof check. Check the enrollment record and registry controls separately.
  4. Retain missing, mismatched and unassessed items. Request the necessary originals or explanation. If evaluation settings change, record the reason and rerun as a separate result.

Do not add a public key from an incoming proof merely to turn the result green. Establish the key-to-customer relationship through your pre-agreed enrollment and authentication process. VeraSnap Verify SDK — device key enrollment

This revision looks up signing keys in the supplied registry. VeraSnap Verify SDK — signer-key lookup and check implementation

3. Separate result colors from business decisions

Reading results from VeraSnap Verify SDK main 66d1875
OutputChecks and next steps
Green / ANCHOR_VERIFIEDDescribes the checks performed. Green is possible without an image input: require media_verified=true and media_hash/passed/match to establish the supplied file's match. It does not prove the incident, scene or photographer's identity.
Yellow / PARTIALLY_VERIFIEDRead the warning: an unknown key and unverified time are different issues. Neither confirmed forgery nor completion of every required check follows from yellow.
Yellow / ANCHOR_PENDINGNo fully verified timestamp. Determine what is missing; do not substitute a planned or expected timestamp for evidence.
Red / HASH_MISMATCH, SIGNATURE_INVALID, ERRORRetain the failed check. Investigate a different file, re-encoding, malformed input or modification. A technical failure alone is not a finding of fraud; request the corresponding original and compare.
skipped / infoDo not count these as successful checks. Record not_provided for a missing photo and attestation/info/stated for attestation merely stated in a proof. These checks do not change the overall color.

Missing timestamps are format-dependent. Synthetic examples at this revision return yellow for an internal proof lacking a token and red when a timestamp is stripped from a shareable proof. Do not assume “no timestamp always means yellow”; inspect checks. Codes and aggregation rules: VeraSnap Verify SDK — result reference

4. Record the limits of what was established

  • File match: matching an input image to a signed hash does not establish whether damage resulted from the claimed incident, or whether a real camera photographed a screen or print.
  • Time: capture_time_device is a device-clock value. A verified RFC 3161 timestamp is evidence about the target digest's existence no later than that time, not independent proof of the capture time itself.
  • People and location: a recorded biometric check does not identify the photographer. GPS is device-reported. Check the customer-to-key relationship and the identity of the subject separately in your business workflow.
  • History: deletion counts and other chain_context information are unsigned context; do not use them as proof that every action was captured.
  • Trust and revocation: this revision does not check TSA CRL/OCSP revocation. Attestation and per-capture assertions stated in a proof are not thereby verified.

VeraSnap Verify SDK — limitations. The supporting work for this guide is a first-party execution of synthetic examples and a review of public materials. It is not independent third-party reproduction, audit or production conformance.

6. Hand off for investigation

Keep mismatching originals. Treat replacement submissions as separate receipts. Do not rewrite a proof or silently alter trust settings to obtain an expected result.

Start a support request with the revision, input format, check IDs/codes and reproduction conditions without personal data. Do not post customer photos, key registries or business credentials in a public issue. Agree an approved channel and scope before sharing confidential material. Contact support.

Give the next operator the execution guide and the versions and known-issues page. Preserve fixed distributions and record a run against a different revision as a separate run.
