AI incident verification records

Before VSO writes about a reported AI incident, it checks the report against primary sources where it can obtain them — court filings, regulators’ releases and the parties’ own statements — and otherwise against the most reliable reporting, and each record says which. This page publishes the records of those checks. Each record states what VSO verified on the date shown and is not updated as a live case tracker. Listing an incident is not a claim about VAP: none of these incidents involved a VAP implementation, and each record states what VAP would not have done.

How to read a record

  • Litigation: every claim made in a case is an untested allegation unless a court has decided it.
  • Jurisdiction: each record is read under its own jurisdiction only. EU law is not applied to matters outside the EU.
  • CONFIRMED — found in the cited sources; a claim confirmed only from secondary reporting is marked “(secondary)”. UNCONFIRMED — not found in the sources checked; this is not a finding that the claim is false. CONTRADICTED — a cited source states otherwise. UNVERIFIABLE — the source could not be retrieved, for example a paywalled court docket.
  • Outcome of the check: VERIFIED — the core claims checked out. PARTIALLY VERIFIED — some did; the rest are marked in the record. PUBLISH WITH CAVEATS — the claims checked out, with limits stated in the record that any use must keep. PUBLISH WITH CORRECTIONS — the report VSO checked contained claims that did not hold up; the summary states only what was verified or attributes it to its source, and the claims that did not hold up are listed with their verdicts. PUBLISH WITH HEAVY CAVEATS — as above, with wider limits; a parenthesis says what may be published in full. PUBLISH AS NEW — the report contained new primary facts and is recorded as its own incident rather than as a follow-up; “after corrections” means corrections were applied first. PROCEDURAL FACTS ONLY — only court and filing facts, and the parties’ public responses, are listed.
  • Registered — entered in VSO’s incident ledger. Verified, pending registration — checked against sources, not yet entered in the ledger.

Method

VSO’s verification is AI-assisted: VSO uses AI models, including Anthropic’s Claude, to search for and compare sources. Each record lists the sources it relies on, linked wherever VSO has confirmed the address, so that readers can check them. Several records involve AI developers. They are held to the same rules and marked: a developer is marked when it is a party, or its model or tool is central to the record, in its role as developer or provider of the AI system at issue. Records involving Anthropic, whose Claude models VSO uses in this work, say so.

Not listed: on hold (4), not recorded as new incidents (no new primary facts) (7), awaiting re-verification (3), registration undecided (1). Follow-up reporting on an incident already listed is not recorded as a new incident.

VAP v1.2 §1.6 (verbatim)

VAP and its domain profiles define mechanisms for producing cryptographically verifiable evidence of AI system decisions. Conformance to VAP or any profile: (a) does not constitute compliance with the EU AI Act, GDPR, MiFID II/III, CAT Rule 613, NIS2, FDA SaMD guidance, or any other law or regulation; (b) does not constitute a legal determination that any technical mechanism (including crypto-shredding) satisfies a specific legal obligation; (c) does not warrant the correctness, fairness, or safety of the underlying AI decisions — only the integrity, completeness (at anchor granularity), and attributability of their records. VAP generates evidence; competent authorities and courts evaluate it.
