When security research is delegated to an AI agent, what usually gets reported is the result: “a problem was found”. This page explains, following the specification VSO publishes (VAP), a design approach that lets a third party check from records how that result was reached — who delegated what, what was observed, and how it was judged. It does not discuss any case involving a specific company.

01 · The problem

A report that says “found it” does not show the whole investigation.

When an AI reports that it has found a problem, that report alone does not tell you:

  • Who authorized which targets and which operations.
  • Which version of the rules applied at run time.
  • How each URL was discovered.
  • What was actually retrieved, and what was inferred.
  • Whether the allow/deny decisions match the traffic that actually took place.
  • Whether, if an assessment was corrected, the original judgment and the reason for the change can be traced.
01

Authority and scope

Who authorized the research, and which targets and operations were allowed? A record that work was delegated to an AI internally and permission obtained from the target organization are different things.

02

Source of observations

From which URL, when, and by what route did the material behind a judgment come? How a URL was found is part of the record.

03

Basis for judgment

Observed facts, referenced information, and conditions not yet confirmed. Unless these three are kept apart, inference travels as if it were fact.

04

What was actually done

Planned operations, allow/deny decisions, and the traffic that actually occurred. Whether decision and execution matched can only be checked by comparing both records.

Five records that cannot stand in for one another

The record that research was authorized, the pre-retrieval decision, the actual traffic, the technical assessment, and the decision on notifying the target company are each a different record.

  1. Authorization recordWho approved which targets, operations and period.
  2. Pre-retrieval decisionHow an operation was allowed or denied before it was performed.
  3. Actual trafficWhat was sent and what was received, including failures and interruptions.
  4. Technical assessmentWhat was concluded from the observations, and what was left unconfirmed.
  5. Notification decisionWhether to inform the target company, and what to tell it.

For example, a record that an operation was decided as “allowed” does not show that the operation was carried out as decided. Conversely, a record of traffic alone does not show whether that traffic stayed within what was authorized.

02 · Combining VAP, OAP and DAP

Linking the provenance of observations with records of delegated actions.

VAP (Verifiable AI Provenance Framework) is a cross-domain framework for making records of AI decisions and actions verifiable after the fact. VAP v1.2 registers OAP, which covers observation of web material published by third parties, and DAP, which covers actions an agent takes under delegated authority. Both are Working Drafts.

Terms used on this page

Provenance
The history of a record or piece of data: when, where, by whom and how it was created and handled.
Delegation
One party (the principal) entrusting work or authority to another party — here, an AI agent — within a defined scope and period.
Verifiability
The property that a third party can check whether records were altered, and how records relate to one another, without having to trust whoever produced them.
External anchoring
Fixing a digest (hash value) of records in an outside service that the record keeper cannot rewrite alone, such as a timestamp authority or a transparency log.
Non-normative
Not a requirement of a specification; reference material to aid understanding.
  • Stated in the specification
    A deployment uses exactly one domain profile and may invoke zero or more cross-cutting capabilities. Each invoked capability must be declared, and where both constrain the same event class, the stricter constraint applies (VAP v1.2 §4.6.3).
  • Stated in the specification
    OAP is described as composing with DAP where retrieval is performed by an autonomous agent (VAP v1.2 §4.6.4 and §5.4).
  • Stated in the specification
    A Working Draft is a stage at which event names, field names and invariant formulations are expected to change; it is not for production conformance claims (VAP v1.2 §4.5.2).
  • Scope of what was checked
    As of this page’s preparation (20 October 2026), the texts of OAP and DAP are not posted in VSO’s public repositories. The explanations on this page are based on the text of VAP v1.2.
Proposed on this page — not official fields defined in OAP or DAP

Additional considerations for security research

For security research, we are considering linking the following information to observation records and delegation records. Which record should carry each item, and in what form, has not been decided.

  • 01Relationship between the asset and its operatorWhich organization the observed domain or service was judged to be operated by, and on what basis.
  • 02How the URL was discoveredWhere the URL came from — a reference in public HTML, DNS, existing observation data whose terms of use were checked, and so on.
  • 03Basis for the product and version observationFrom which part of which response a product name or version number was read.
  • 04Advisories referencedThe source of the vulnerability information used for matching, and its version at the time it was consulted.
  • 05Unconfirmed conditionsConditions that cannot be confirmed from outside, such as the version of the code actually running, individual patches, or whether a feature is in use.
  • 06Correction history of the assessmentWhen an assessment changes: the original assessment, the new one, the reason, and who made the change.

What the specification states, and what this page proposes

The left side of each row is what VAP v1.2 states; the right side is this page’s own application proposal. The right side is not a definition in OAP, DAP or VAP.

Observation records

Stated in the specification

OAP is a domain profile for the provenance of records of web material published by third parties and observed at a URL (§2.2.6, §5.4). It refers to an ObservationScopeManifest and to an attempt-closure invariant, Invariant OAP-1 (§5.4; their definitions are in the OAP text).

Proposed on this page

Link how each URL was discovered, and the basis for each product and version observation, to the observation record.

Delegation records

Stated in the specification

DAP is a cross-cutting capability for the provenance of agent access, tool invocation, action and data transfer under authority delegated by a principal (§4.6.2). VAP’s responsibility schema includes a delegation_chain of delegator, delegatee, scope and validity period (§4.4.3).

Proposed on this page

Treat internal delegation to an agent and permission obtained from the target organization as separate records.

Composition

Stated in the specification

A deployment consists of one domain profile and zero or more declared capabilities; where constraints overlap, the stricter applies (§4.6.3). OAP composes with DAP where retrieval is performed by an autonomous agent (§4.6.4, §5.4).

Proposed on this page

Link OAP and DAP records along six research stages: setting the scope, delegation, pre-retrieval decision, observation and retrieval, technical assessment, and notification, correction and handover.

Version of the rules

Stated in the specification

VAP’s policy identification (policy_id, §7.1) identifies the verification policy under which events were produced. It does not represent the rules for what research may or may not do.

Proposed on this page

Reference the version of the research rules (targets, permitted and prohibited operations, period of application) from the delegation record and the pre-retrieval decision record.

Detecting alteration and omission

Stated in the specification

External anchoring is required at all conformance levels (INT-006). For any anchored batch, a third party must be able to detect omission or split-view presentation after anchoring (Completeness Invariant, INT-008). Events that were never recorded are out of reach (Appendix A).

Proposed on this page

When verification is implemented, state the trusted record source, how records are fixed externally, and the scope being verified.

Assessment, notification and correction

Stated in the specification

There is an abstract provenance model (actor, input, context, action, outcome; §4.2.2) and an override_history (§4.4.3). The VAP v1.2 text defines no records specific to security assessments or notification decisions (the OAP and DAP texts have not been checked).

Proposed on this page

Treat observed facts, referenced information, likely applicability, unconfirmed conditions, the notification decision and the reason for a correction as separate records.

Scope of assurance

Stated in the specification

Conformance to VAP or a profile does not constitute compliance with any law and does not warrant that recorded decisions are correct, fair or safe; it concerns only the integrity, completeness at anchor granularity, and attributability of the records (§1.6). VAP does not prevent, block or intercept AI behaviour (Declaration).

Proposed on this page

Design the evidence mechanism and execution control as separate mechanisms.

Internal delegation and the target organization’s permission are different things

Internal delegation

Within the organization doing the research: who entrusted which agent with what scope of work. Changes to and revocation of the delegation are recorded too. The intent is to map this to what DAP covers — actions under delegated authority (proposed on this page).

≠

The target organization’s permission

Which scope and period of testing the target company has authorized. This is a separate record, linked to the version of the authorization letter or contract.

Internal delegation creates no authority to access third-party systems. Operations that presuppose the target organization’s permission (for example, additional testing under a permission or contract) are linked to the record of that permission; an internal delegation record is never used in its place.

Keep execution control and the evidence mechanism separate

Execution control

Execution control enforces the scope of operations in the system.

Evidence mechanism

The evidence mechanism produces records of those decisions and of their execution that can be checked afterwards.

Evidence records alone cannot prevent prohibited operations. VAP is a framework for after-the-fact verification and does not prevent, block or intercept AI behaviour (VAP v1.2, Declaration). Preventing prohibited operations requires separate execution control.

What to state if verification is implemented

If detection of alteration, omission or substitution is implemented in future, at least the following three points will be stated (proposed on this page).

Trusted record source

Whose records, signed with which keys, serve as the starting point for verification.

How records are fixed externally

In which outside service (a timestamp authority, a transparency log, and so on) record digests are fixed, and how often. In VAP v1.2, external anchoring is required at all conformance levels (INT-006).

Scope of verification

Which batches, periods and kinds of record were verified. Post-anchor omission or split-view presentation can be within reach of detection; actions that were never recorded cannot be detected (VAP v1.2 §4.1.7, Appendix A).

VAP v1.2 recommends an Evidence Pack: a bundle a third party can verify offline, containing events, audit paths, anchor records, external anchor proofs, public keys, policy documents and similar material (§9.3). The example on this page is not an Evidence Pack.

05 · What can be verified, and the limits

Being able to verify the evidence is not the same as the judgment being right.

These are the premises for evaluating this evidence design. Read them as part of the design.

What this design aims to do, and what that alone does not guarantee
What the design aims to doWhat that alone does not guarantee
Detect alteration of stored recordsThat observations represent reality completely and accurately
Trace judgments and their basisThat a vulnerability exists, or that it can be exploited
Cross-check delegation records against operation recordsThat the target company gave permission, or that the research was lawful
Verify evidence within a declared scopeThat no unmeasured or unrecorded action took place

Adding a hash later cannot prove a past approval

A hash value confirms that the content of a document is unchanged; it does not show when the document was created. To show that an approval existed at a given time, a record of that approval must be included in records that were fixed externally at that time. Adding a hash to a document created or obtained afterwards cannot meet this condition. And even an externally fixed record does not show that the approver had authority, or that what was approved was appropriate.

This proposal is an evidence design for verifying research records after the fact. It does not grant access to any target, and it does not guarantee that a vulnerability exists, legal compliance, or independent certification.

Position in the specification: conformance to VAP or a profile does not constitute legal compliance and does not warrant that recorded decisions are correct (VAP v1.2 §1.6). Events that were never measured are, by construction, outside the reach of verification (VAP v1.2, Appendix A).

Review the application design

We welcome comments on this application design, for example on:

  • Whether the distinction between the five records (authorization, pre-retrieval decision, actual traffic, technical assessment, notification decision) is sufficient.
  • Which OAP or DAP records the additional considerations should be linked to.
  • Whether the premises of verification (trusted record source, how records are fixed externally, scope of verification) are stated clearly enough.

Comments do not amount to normative changes to VAP or any profile. Specification changes follow VSO’s change management process (VAP v1.2 §10.3).
