VSO · 2026-11-14 · SOURCE REVIEW

Snowball and Grok Build reports: AI actions, publication permissions and evidence limits

VSO reviewed the 13 November monitoring note against primary sources available on 14 November. Incident dates, source publication dates and this article’s publication date are distinct. Reading investigators’ reports is not reproduction in affected environments.

Experimental extensions remain unadopted; independent third-party reproduction has not been established. First-party CI success is distinct from independent fresh execution. This article establishes no VAP/VCP prevention record, full conformance or certification for these cases.

1. Operation Snowball

Primary-source reports

Malbear Labs reported on 8 November that recovered records showed over two weeks of AI-agent intrusion activity against a European telecom. Discovery was in November; a pre-November attack period is not established. Access to millions of patient records was reported, not confirmed exfiltration of all records or successful compromise of all 13 associated organizations. Malbear Labs · 2026-11-08

On 13 November at 17:38 GMT, Cisco corrected its CVE-2026-20320 advisory: it has no direct evidence of active exploitation. This is distinct from CISA’s 10 November Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) listing. Neither establishes Cisco attribution of the vulnerability to Snowball. Cisco · 2026-11-13 · CISA · 2026-11-10

Researcher estimates and unresolved points

These are investigators’ accounts of recovered records. VSO has not independently validated victim environments or the complete underlying records, and does not establish total impact or the extent of human involvement.

2. Grok Build / GhostShares

Primary-source reports

Claroc reported on 10 November that 35,578 of 37,571 online apps opened without sign-in—not that all contained confidential data. Publication required user action; the broad default was observed in its tested account. Write/delete authorization flaws were demonstrated only in researcher-owned apps. Claroc Research · 2026-11-10

Researcher estimates and unresolved points

The 650–800 private-data apps are an estimate. Its relationship to the separate “about one in ten” sensitive-data statement is unclear; this is not a confirmed victim count. Removal of 24 provider apps and incomplete action for other users describe the report’s 10 November status. Claroc · Methods / Response

VSO analysis — not adoption or demonstrated results

Snowball raises evidence questions: can human instructions, AI decisions, delegation and execution results be distinguished and compared with operation and network records preserved under separate administration? Complete, accurate records supplied by an attacker cannot be assumed.

Grok Build raises questions about recording publication approval and permission changes, while checking authentication and authorization separately. A valid signature does not establish proper authorization or safety. These are topics for VAP review, not demonstrated direct VCP applicability or incident-prevention capability.

VAP Limits

Sources and review scope

This is a public-source review. VSO did not sign in to or operate affected apps, or reproduce the attacks. Later changes in exposure and impact need separate verification.

Historical test records retain their original revisions and conditions. This addition is limited to a review of public reports and analysis; normative requirements, existing implementations, signed data, fixed distributions and K3 are unchanged.
